draws, the data frames it returns, the coefficients and test results it

This matters here in a way it does not for every library: what comes out of
qdaR is figures and numbers that go into papers, theses and reports.  A
licensing question hanging over a figure in a submitted manuscript is exactly
the kind of doubt nobody needs.


2. Where parts of the package end up inside your output

Producing a result sometimes means copying fragments of the package into it:
the axis labels and legend titles of a figure, the wording of a caption, the
colour values of the default palette, the column names of a returned data
frame, and the text of the messages and warnings the package emits.  For
exactly those fragments you are granted an unlimited, irrevocable permission
to use, modify and distribute them as part of your output, under terms of your

That permission covers them as part of your output.  It is not a licence to

3. What this does not cover

The reference files installed in inst/extdata -- the exchange contract and its
sample exports -- are part of the package, not output you produced.  They are
covered by the AGPL like the rest of it.  In practice this changes nothing for
anyone analysing their own data.


4. Why this clause exists

Strictly speaking it changes nothing.  The GNU licences have never covered the
output of a program -- images made with GIMP belong to the person who drew
them, and the Free Software Foundation says so explicitly in its GPL FAQ.  The
belief that a copyleft licence reaches into a program's results is a
persistent myth.

But a myth is enough to make an ethics board, a university legal office or a
publisher hesitate, and qualitative research data is sensitive enough without
a licensing question hanging over it.  This clause removes the doubt in
writing.

The same permission applies to zotQDA, to qdaZ and to the Python twin qdaPy,
in the same words.  In those projects it lives in a file named
LICENSE-EXCEPTION.md; here it is part of LICENSE, because that is where an R
package states terms additional to a standard licence.
